Key Advantages

Why Choose Engineered Stone

From homogeneous raw material to invisible jointing, engineered stone outperforms natural stone on consistency and design freedom.

🔬

Homogeneous, Consistent Batch to Batch

Factory-controlled formulation means one colour code stays visually identical across the whole slab — no veining surprises, no fissures.

🧩

Seamless Jointing, One Continuous Piece

Dedicated adhesive and graded sanding and polishing leave joints with continuous pattern and no detectable touch line across long runs.

🛡️

Stain Resistant, Virtually Non-porous

Negligible capillary pores and water absorption as low as 0.02% keep soy sauce, red wine, coffee and oil from penetrating.

🦠

Hygienic, No Pores to Trap Dirt

A pore-free surface does not harbour mould or bacteria, making it suitable for food contact, healthcare and laboratory areas.

♻️

Thermoformable and Renovate-able

Acrylic surfaces can be thermoformed into curves and integrated basins; scuffed areas can be sanded back to like-new.

🎨

Controllable Palette, Repeatable Supply

Colour codes can be re-supplied long term — ideal for chains, hotels and multi-unit projects where colour matching really matters.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions we are asked most often before a specification is confirmed.

How is engineered stone different from natural stone?

Engineered stone is produced from natural quartz, mineral filler and resin under factory-controlled heat and pressure, so it has none of the fissures, colour variation or inclusions of natural stone. It is homogeneous, has very low water absorption and high stain resistance, and can be fabricated into seamless long runs and integrated basins. Natural stone wins on one-of-a-kind veining; engineered stone wins on consistency and design freedom — the two are often combined in the same space.

Are the seams really invisible?

Yes. We use a colour-matched dedicated adhesive, align the pattern geometrically before bonding, then sand through a graded sequence and polish. The result is a joint where pattern and sheen continue across the bond line. Honed finishes and solid colours hide joints best; for strongly veined varieties we lay out the pattern first so the veining reads naturally across the join.

Is engineered stone suitable for a kitchen worktop?

Very much so. Quartz varieties absorb as little as 0.02% water and resist scratching and staining, which is why they dominate kitchen worktops and islands. One caveat: varieties with higher resin content do not tolerate prolonged high heat, so use a trivet rather than placing a hot pan directly on the surface.

Quartz or acrylic solid surface — which should I choose?

It depends on the brief. For hardness, scratch resistance and value on a straightforward worktop, choose quartz (Mohs 6–7, absorption ≤0.02%). For seamless sculptural work, thermoformed curves, integrated basins, or a surface that can be sanded back later, choose acrylic solid surface. The two are often combined in one kitchen — quartz for the worktop, acrylic for the bowl and curved sections.

Can you fabricate to my drawings, and how long does it take?

Yes — we fabricate to drawings and site dimensions, including waterjet cut-outs, shaped profiles, thermoformed curves and integrated basins. Standard sizes in stock colour codes take roughly 2–3 weeks; thermoforming, special colour codes or large project volumes take roughly 3–5 weeks, confirmed against the production schedule once drawings are approved.
