02Product Range & Selection

We supply natural stone from 60+ countries including Italy, Greece, Turkey and Brazil, covering marble, granite, limestone, travertine, sandstone, onyx and luxury stone across 300+ regularly stocked varieties. We can also match a sample or photograph against our global quarry database.

Marble offers decorative veining and suits aesthetic-focused interiors such as hotel lobbies and villa feature walls. Granite is harder, more wear-resistant and weather-resistant, making it the choice for high-traffic malls, airports, metro stations and exterior cladding. Use granite for heavy wear and outdoor areas; reserve marble for visual focal points and low-abrasion zones.

Interior dry-hang wall cladding is typically 18–20mm; flooring starts at 20mm, rising to 25–30mm for heavy traffic or load-bearing areas. Countertops and furniture panels commonly use 20mm with a thickened edge. Exterior cladding thickness follows wind-load calculation and local codes, often 25–30mm, or a lightweight honeycomb composite alternative.

Variation in colour and veining is inherent to natural stone. We cut from the same quarry face and the same block batch, then sort, number and dry-lay slabs by tone and vein direction before dispatch, keeping colour difference within the internationally accepted range (ΔE < 2.0) and submitting a layout drawing for approval where required.

Book-matching mirrors two adjacent slabs along a central axis to create symmetrical butterfly veining, often used for lobby feature walls. Vein-matching aligns multiple slabs in sequence so the veining runs continuously across the surface. Both require dedicated production planning at the cutting stage, so please specify them at drawing stage.

We offer polished, honed, leathered, flamed, bush-hammered, sandblasted, antique and brushed finishes among 10+ options, customised to the design style, required slip rating and maintenance conditions of each space.

For wet areas, pool surrounds and outdoor steps we recommend textured finishes such as flamed, bush-hammered or sandblasted, with dynamic coefficient of friction (DCOF) measured to ANSI A326.3. Polished or honed finishes suit dry interior areas. We recommend a slip class based on usage and local code and provide test data.

Natural stone radioactivity is extremely low. Every batch is tested for radionuclide limits in accordance with GB 6566 and complies with Class A decorative material requirements, making it suitable for residences, schools and hospitals. Third-party test reports are available on request.

Natural stone is our core business, but we can advise on quartz, porcelain slab and honeycomb composite alternatives, comparing veining, physical performance, maintenance cost and budget to help you reach the best specification decision.

Yes. Send a physical sample or high-resolution photos and we will match against our quarry database by base colour, vein direction, crystal structure and gloss level. Candidate materials and pricing are usually returned within 3–7 working days; rare stones can trigger a global search.

For countertops we recommend dense, low-absorption granite or quartzitic stone, particularly darker granites. Marble countertops require penetrating sealers and will develop patina. In wet bathroom areas prioritise slip rating and acid resistance, avoiding limestone and travertine which are sensitive to acidic cleaners.

Luxury stone refers to scarce varieties with rare veining, very limited extraction and gem-like or translucent quality, usually priced per slab and requiring dedicated selection. Standard marble offers stable supply and accessible pricing for large areas. The two work well together: luxury stone for focal points, standard material for the field.

08Installation & Site Support

We provide technical support and installation guidance, and can deploy technicians for site briefing and inspection, while laying and installation are performed by the client or a professional installation team. We work with quality installers in many regions and can recommend suitable teams for your location.

Interior walls typically use dry-hanging (stainless steel brackets or undercut anchors) or thin-set adhesive fixing. Floors generally use mortar bed or thin-set adhesive. Exterior cladding mainly uses undercut anchor dry-hanging with wind-load and seismic calculation. Method selection depends on substrate, height and code requirements.

Verify substrate flatness and strength, complete waterproofing acceptance, set out lines and confirm the layout, then dry-lay delivered material to check colour and vein direction. Prepare matching adhesive, grout, sealer and specialised tools in advance rather than sourcing mid-installation.

Clean off adhesive residue and cement slurry promptly, cover surfaces with protective film or board to prevent contamination and impact from follow-on trades, never drag heavy objects or cut directly on stone surfaces, and fit corner protection on steps and corners until final acceptance.

Interior floors generally need 1–2mm joints; outdoor and large areas require wider joints and division joints per code to absorb thermal movement and structural deformation. Use a low-shrinkage, stain-resistant grout colour-matched to the stone, and avoid acidic products.

Hollowing usually results from poor substrate preparation, incomplete adhesive coverage or loading during curing. Minor hollowing can be remedied by grout injection; severe cases require removal and re-laying. Prevention lies in substrate cleaning, correct adhesive selection, proper thin-set pressing technique and adequate curing time.

The key is pre-dispatch dry-lay and numbering: install strictly in numbered sequence without swapping, use one batch per area, and unify light direction and vein orientation per the design drawing. Never mix batches within a single area.

Wind-load, seismic and thermal calculations are required; brackets and anchors should be stainless steel with anti-corrosion treatment; panels need six-sided protection; joints must allow drainage and movement. For high-rise projects consider lightweight honeycomb composite panels and verify with pull-out testing.

After the adhesive bed has fully cured and the stone is completely dry — typically 7–14 days depending on humidity. Clean the surface and ensure it is free of stains and residual moisture before applying penetrating sealer to guarantee performance.

Yes. We supply installation instructions, layout drawings, numbered schedules and a maintenance manual with each shipment, and can arrange online or on-site technical briefings covering adhesive selection, movement joint detailing, protection processes and finished-surface protection.

09Care, Maintenance & Restoration

Wipe with a slightly damp mop or a neutral cleaner (pH 6–8). Avoid cleaners containing hydrochloric or hydrofluoric acid or strong alkali. Remove grit promptly to reduce abrasion and dry surfaces after cleaning to prevent water spotting.

It depends on use and traffic: interior floors generally every 1–2 years, wet areas, countertops and exterior areas every 6–12 months. Use the water droplet test — if water no longer beads but is quickly absorbed, it is time to re-seal.

Blot surface liquid first, then treat with a neutral cleaner or a dedicated stone poultice. Oil stains respond to poulticing with a degreasing paste sealed for 24 hours. Deep ink contamination needs professional treatment. Never use strong acid or alkali, which causes permanent etching and dulling.

Slight dulling can be treated with polishing powder or crystallisation agents. Moderate wear requires professional grinding and polishing, progressing from coarse to fine abrasives. Severe wear can be restored by refinishing to recover flatness and gloss without replacing the material.

Water spots are darker marks from moisture retained in the stone, treated by drying, improved ventilation or a specialist water-spot remover. Efflorescence is a white powdery deposit: remove the crystals, apply back-coating and surface sealer, and investigate the moisture source and drainage.

Marble reacts with acids such as lemon juice, vinegar, soft drinks and some cleaners, causing etching and dulling. Wipe acidic liquids promptly, use boards and coasters, choose neutral cleaners and re-apply penetrating sealer periodically. Light etch marks can be polished out.

Stone conducts heat well and suits underfloor heating, but choose dense low-absorption stone, use flexible adhesive and grout, raise temperature gradually (within 5°C per day on first start-up) and provide adequate movement joints to avoid thermal stress cracking.

Use weather-resistant granite or suitably treated travertine outdoors, apply six-sided protection and adequate drainage falls to avoid standing water. In cold climates verify freeze-thaw resistance to local standards and inspect sealers and joints regularly, re-applying as needed.

Daily cleaning and small-area polishing are fine to do yourself. Grinding and levelling, joint repair, deep stain removal and large-area crystallisation are best left to professionals, since incorrect abrasive selection or localised over-grinding can cause irreversible waviness and uneven gloss.

Routine cleaning costs very little. Professional crystallisation, polishing and sealing are priced by area depending on region and stone condition, and are far cheaper than replacement. Regular maintenance extends service life by decades, giving a clear whole-life cost advantage.

Avoid strong acid cleaners containing hydrochloric, hydrofluoric or phosphoric acid, heavy-duty alkaline degreasers, bleach and abrasive powders, as well as all-purpose cleaners of unknown composition. Choose products labelled safe for natural stone and neutral in pH.

Clean thoroughly and seal before vacancy, maintain ventilation and moderate dehumidification, avoid prolonged direct sunlight and long-term sealed plastic covering which traps moisture haze, and ventilate and inspect monthly to prevent mould and efflorescence.
