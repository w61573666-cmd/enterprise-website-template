How to Choose a Kitchen Countertop: Marble vs Granite vs Quartz vs Sintered Stone

Home › Stone Guides › How to Choose a Kitchen Countertop: Marble vs Granite vs Quartz vs Sintered Stone

The countertop is the hardest-working surface in your kitchen, so the stone has to balance hardness, heat resistance, stain resistance, looks and budget. This guide compares the four most popular countertop materials in Hong Kong — marble, granite, quartz and sintered stone — so you can match the stone to how you actually cook and live.

MarbleMohs 3–4 · luxury veining · needs care
GraniteMohs 6–7 · heat-proof · classic HK
QuartzMohs 6–7 · stain-proof · low care
SinteredResin-free · UV-stable · thin slabs

The four materials at a glance

Marble is a metamorphic calcium-carbonate rock at Mohs 3–4 — luxurious but acid-sensitive and maintenance-aware. Granite is a natural igneous rock at Mohs 6–7, dense, heat- and wear-resistant. Quartz is over 90% natural quartz sand bound with resin, Mohs 6–7 with absorption as low as 0.02%. Sintered stone is 100% natural minerals fired above 1200°C — no resin and UV-stable.

Marble: the most luxurious look, with care

Marble’s unique veining makes it the dream-kitchen favourite, but at Mohs 3–4 it etches on contact with acids such as lemon or vinegar. Use chopping boards and trivets and apply a penetrating sealer. Best for design-led households who cook moderately and are happy to maintain the surface.

Granite: the hard-wearing classic

Granite sits at Mohs 6–7, is naturally dense and tolerates direct heat, with dark varieties performing especially well against stains — the traditional, high-value Hong Kong kitchen choice. Its grainy, speckled look offers less variety than marble, and sealing after installation is recommended.

Quartz: the low-maintenance favourite

Quartz offers a huge range of patterns, seamless long runs, ultra-low absorption and easy cleaning with almost no maintenance — one of the most popular choices in Hong Kong today. Its resin content means very hot pots should sit on a trivet rather than directly on the surface.

Sintered stone: the new-generation option

Sintered stone contains no resin, comes in lightweight slabs up to 3.2 m with continuous veining, and resists scratches, heat and UV — it even wraps cabinet doors and works on balconies. Joints cannot be made fully invisible, and the hard, brittle slab demands skilled CNC fabrication and installation, so choose a supplier with its own workshop.

Match the stone to your household

Daily wok cooking: granite or sintered stone for heat resistance. Kids-first, easy cleaning: quartz or sintered stone. Natural luxury look: marble with sealing and good habits. Budget-first: entry marble or granite. Matching worktops and cabinet doors: thin sintered stone slabs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is best for an open Hong Kong kitchen?

For easy cleaning and durability, quartz or sintered stone; for natural luxury veining, marble with sealing and careful cleaning; for heat-resistant value, granite. Open kitchens put joints on show — quartz offers seamless joints while sintered stone minimises them with very large slabs.

Is quartz “chemical” and less natural?

Quartz is over 90% natural quartz sand, bound with a small amount of resin; it is safe for indoor tops and passes food-contact tests. If you want 100% resin-free and formaldehyde-free, consider sintered stone or natural marble/granite.

What thickness should I choose?

Hong Kong kitchen tops are typically 20mm, thickened to 30–40mm where a substantial edge is preferred. Cantilevered breakfast bars or wide islands need battens or supports sized to the span — your consultant calculates this at the on-site measurement.
