Carrara, Italy; density 2.70g/cm³; water absorption ~0.20%; bold veining — first choice for book-matched walls

Carrara White

Italy; density 2.71g/cm³; absorption ~0.30%; the most stable choice for large wall and floor areas

Portoro Black

Rare Italian source; density 2.72g/cm³; absorption ~0.15%; the premium accent material

Azul Macaubas

Brazil; density 2.65g/cm³; absorption ~0.25%; a statement luxury stone for lobbies and clubs

Roman Travertine

Premium Turkish quarries; density 2.35g/cm³; higher absorption requires enhanced sealing; ideal for facades

Pitfalls to Avoid

Material Selection Pitfalls

Judging by samples only

Small samples mislead — always review full slabs or the placement drawing before approval

Ignoring vein direction

Horizontal vs vertical veining changes the whole space; confirm direction before ordering

No vein-match allowance

Book-matching adds 5–10% waste over standard cuts — budget quantity accordingly

Finish mismatched to use

Polished floors in wet areas are slippery — match finish to function

Lighting as an afterthought

Stone reads differently at each color temperature — involve lighting design early

Price Factors

What Drives the Price

Stone & quarry

Rare and imported stones command premium unit prices; quality domestic alternatives control budget

Size & thickness

Large and thick slabs consume more raw block — size optimization cuts cost directly

Surface finish

Polished is the baseline; flamed and bush-hammered carry process premiums — mix flexibly

Custom complexity

Curves, patterns and moldings priced by difficulty — simplified profiles balance effect and cost
