Color Variance Control System

Large-area installations fear color drift. Where does it come from, and how do we lock it down with batch locking, graded inspection and layout preview? This page shows the whole system.

Batch Locking / Graded Inspection / Layout Preview
Where Variance Comes From

Where Color Variance Originates

Quarry and stratum differences

Blocks from different strata of the same mountain differ — mixing quarries is the number-one cause of visible drift

Batch time gaps

Blocks cut at different times color-shift naturally; buying in batches without locking them guarantees mismatch

Cutting direction

With-grain and cross-grain cuts read differently — inconsistent direction amplifies perceived variance

Finish processes

Polished and flamed reflect light differently — mixing finishes creates false variance

Laying direction & lighting

The same batch laid differently or lit differently looks different

Sealing & staining

Uneven sealing or later staining causes localized color change

Triple Lock

The Triple Lock Mechanism

① Batch locked in contract

Quarry and block batch written into the contract; any cross-batch requires written approval — no mixing at the source

② Piece-by-piece graded inspection

Every piece inspected to the contracted grading standard, shipped same-grade with labels — no grade mixing

③ Production photo approval

Production-line photos and video before packing — goods ship only after your approval

Grading Standard

The Grading Standard

Industry grading system

Goods shipped by variance grade (match-grade / tolerance-grade) with the standard written into the contract

Colorimeter support

Key projects quantified with colorimeter ΔE data — managed by numbers, not feel

International standards

Inspection to ASTM or your specified standard

SGS third-party recheck

On-arrival third-party recheck supported; reports form the acceptance basis

Layout Preview

The Layout Preview System

Slab scanning

Every slab high-res scanned and archived — texture and tone managed digitally

Piece-numbered layouts

Book-match placement drawings from scans; every piece uniquely numbered

Pre-laying confirmation

Premium projects get pre-laying photos or video for review before shipping

Numbered installation

Sites lay by number — the wall matches the placement drawing exactly

Multi-Section Management

Multi-Section Unified Management

Independent per-tower batches

Each tower batch-numbered with archived samples — cross-tower comparisons have evidence

Junctions flagged in advance

Connected lobbies and echoing lift lobbies flagged early for unified control

Same-batch spares priority

Later spares drawn from archived project stock for consistent color

One acceptance standard

All sections accepted to a single grading standard — no standard drift
